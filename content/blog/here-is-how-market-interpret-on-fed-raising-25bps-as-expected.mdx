## The 25bps Hike: Expected But Still Meaningful

When the Federal Reserve raises its benchmark interest rate by 25 basis points (0.25%), markets react — even when the move was fully anticipated. An "expected" rate hike is priced into asset values, but the Fed's accompanying statement, press conference, and forward guidance often contain surprises that drive market movement.

## How to Read the Fed's Message

The rate decision itself is just the headline. What matters more:

**1. The Dot Plot**
The "dot plot" shows where each Fed official expects rates to be at year-end. If the median dot moves up (suggesting more hikes ahead), that's hawkish — negative for equities. If it moves down, that's dovish — positive.

**2. The Statement Language**
Key phrases to watch:
- "Ongoing increases" → more hikes coming (hawkish)
- "Some additional firming" → fewer hikes ahead (slightly dovish)
- "Monitoring incoming data" → data-dependent, possibly pausing

**3. Powell's Press Conference**
The Fed Chair's tone and answers to questions reveal more than the written statement. Watch for softening language about the labor market or inflation — these hint at a pivot.

## Market Reactions to Different Scenarios

| Scenario | Equity Reaction | Bond Reaction |
|---|---|---|
| 25bps + dovish statement | Rally | Bonds rise (yields fall) |
| 25bps + hawkish statement | Selloff | Bonds fall (yields rise) |
| 25bps + in-line statement | Flat / muted | Stable |
| Pause (no hike) | Strong rally | Bonds rally |

## Sector Playbook

**If the Fed signals fewer hikes ahead (dovish):**
- Buy: Technology, Growth stocks, Real Estate
- Avoid: Bank stocks (net interest margin may compress sooner)

**If the Fed signals more hikes ahead (hawkish):**
- Buy: Financials (banks benefit from higher rates), Energy, Consumer Staples
- Reduce: High-P/E growth stocks, long-duration bonds

## The "Buy the News" Phenomenon

Markets often rally into a widely expected Fed hike (buy the rumor) and then sell off immediately after (sell the news) — or vice versa. This counterintuitive reaction happens because expectations are already priced in.

If the actual hike and statement are exactly as expected, there's nothing new to react to — so short-term traders take profits and the market often reverses direction briefly.

## Key Takeaway

A 25bps hike "as expected" tells you less about market direction than the Fed's forward guidance. Focus your attention on the dot plot and Powell's tone — that's where the real information lives. Match your sector positioning to the implied future rate path.
