## What is the Hammer Candlestick?

The **hammer** is one of the most widely recognized bullish reversal patterns in technical analysis. It gets its name from its shape — a small body at the top with a long lower shadow, resembling a hammer.

It signals that sellers drove the price down sharply during the session, but buyers stepped in with enough force to push it back up near the opening price.

## Anatomy of a Hammer

A valid hammer has:
- **Small real body** at the upper end of the trading range
- **Long lower shadow** — at least 2x the length of the body
- **Little or no upper shadow**
- **Color doesn't matter** — green (bullish) is slightly stronger than red (bearish)

The longer the lower shadow relative to the body, the stronger the rejection of lower prices.

## Why the Hammer Works

The psychology behind the pattern:
1. Price opens and sellers push it down aggressively (long lower wick)
2. At some point, buyers overwhelm sellers and push price back up
3. Price closes near or above the open
4. The long lower shadow shows sellers tried and failed — buyers are gaining control

This shift in control from sellers to buyers is what gives the hammer its predictive power.

## Hammer vs Hanging Man

The same candle shape can be bearish depending on context:
- **Hammer** — appears after a downtrend = bullish signal
- **Hanging Man** — appears after an uptrend = bearish signal

Context is everything. The pattern is identical; only the trend it appears in differs.

## Trading the Hammer

**Entry:** Wait for confirmation — the next candle should close higher than the hammer's close. Don't jump in on the hammer alone.

**Stop Loss:** Place below the hammer's low. If price breaks that level, the reversal thesis is invalid.

**Target:** The nearest resistance level, or a 2:1 reward-to-risk ratio.

**Volume:** A hammer on high volume is significantly more reliable than one on low volume. High volume confirms real buying interest.

## Where to Look for Hammers

Hammers are most meaningful at:
- **Key support levels** — price has been rejected here before
- **Round numbers** — $100, $50, $200 act as psychological support
- **Moving averages** — 50-day or 200-day MA
- **Fibonacci retracement levels** — 38.2%, 50%, 61.8%

## Key Takeaway

The hammer is a powerful signal but not infallible. Always wait for confirmation, place your stop below the wick, and look for hammers at meaningful price levels rather than in random locations on a chart.
