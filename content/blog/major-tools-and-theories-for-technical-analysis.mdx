## The Foundation of Technical Analysis

Technical analysis is built on the belief that all known information is reflected in price, and that price patterns repeat because human psychology doesn't change. The goal: identify recurring patterns to forecast future price movement.

## Core Theories

### Dow Theory
The oldest and most foundational theory in technical analysis, developed by Charles Dow in the late 1800s.

Key principles:
- **Markets discount everything** — all known information is priced in
- **Three trend types:** Primary (months to years), Secondary (weeks to months), Minor (days to weeks)
- **Three market phases:** Accumulation, Public Participation, Distribution
- **Volume confirms trends** — rising price on rising volume = valid trend
- **Averages must confirm each other** — Dow and Transportation indexes should confirm each other's signals

### Elliott Wave Theory
Developed by Ralph Nelson Elliott in the 1930s. Markets move in predictable wave patterns driven by collective investor psychology.

Structure:
- **5-wave impulse pattern** in the direction of the trend
- **3-wave corrective pattern** against the trend
- Waves subdivide into smaller waves of the same pattern (fractal nature)

Elliott Wave is complex and subjective — two analysts can see different wave counts on the same chart. Best used as a framework for understanding market psychology rather than a precise trading system.

## Key Technical Tools

### Support and Resistance
- **Support:** A price level where buying has historically emerged — a floor
- **Resistance:** A price level where selling has historically emerged — a ceiling
- When support breaks, it often becomes resistance (and vice versa)

### Trendlines
Connect a series of higher lows (uptrend) or lower highs (downtrend). A break of a trendline signals potential trend change.

### Moving Averages
- **Simple Moving Average (SMA):** Equal weight to all periods
- **Exponential Moving Average (EMA):** More weight to recent prices
- Common periods: 20, 50, 100, 200 days
- The **200-day MA** is watched by virtually all professional traders as the long-term trend indicator

### Relative Strength Index (RSI)
Measures the speed and magnitude of price moves. Developed by J. Welles Wilder. Overbought above 70, oversold below 30.

### MACD
Moving Average Convergence Divergence. Shows trend direction and momentum shifts through the relationship between 12-day and 26-day EMAs.

### Fibonacci Retracements
Based on the Fibonacci sequence, key retracement levels (23.6%, 38.2%, 50%, 61.8%, 78.6%) are widely watched as potential support/resistance zones after a trend move.

## Key Takeaway

No single tool predicts the future. Technical analysis works best when multiple tools point to the same conclusion. Learn a few tools deeply rather than many tools superficially.
