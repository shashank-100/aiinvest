## What is Macroeconomics?

**Macroeconomics** studies the economy as a whole — national output, employment, inflation, interest rates, and international trade. For investors, understanding macro forces is essential because they drive the overall direction of financial markets.

Even the best individual stock can be dragged down by a deteriorating macro environment. And mediocre companies can rise in a strong macro tide.

## The Key Macroeconomic Variables

### Inflation
Inflation measures the rate at which prices rise over time. The US Federal Reserve targets 2% annual inflation as healthy.

**Impact on stocks:**
- **Moderate inflation (1-3%):** Generally positive. Indicates economic growth, companies can raise prices.
- **High inflation (5%+):** Negative. Erodes purchasing power, forces interest rate hikes, compresses stock valuations.
- **Deflation:** Also dangerous. Signals weak demand, falling corporate revenues.

The **Consumer Price Index (CPI)** and **Personal Consumption Expenditures (PCE)** are the two key inflation measures the Fed watches.

### Interest Rates
The Federal Reserve sets the **Federal Funds Rate** — the baseline interest rate for the US economy.

**Impact on stocks:**
- **Rising rates:** Negative for growth stocks (future earnings worth less when discounted at higher rates). Positive for banks and financial stocks.
- **Falling rates:** Positive for growth stocks and real estate. Lower borrowing costs boost corporate profits.

The relationship between interest rates and stock valuations is one of the most important in finance. Higher rates = lower P/E multiples the market is willing to pay.

### GDP Growth
**Gross Domestic Product (GDP)** measures the total economic output of a country. Healthy GDP growth (2-3% annually in the US) supports corporate revenue growth.

- **Expansion:** Rising GDP, low unemployment, growing corporate profits
- **Recession:** Two consecutive quarters of negative GDP growth. Corporate revenues fall, layoffs increase.

## The Business Cycle

The economy moves through four phases:
1. **Expansion:** GDP rising, unemployment falling, inflation picking up
2. **Peak:** Maximum economic output, inflation often high
3. **Contraction/Recession:** GDP falling, unemployment rising
4. **Trough:** Bottom of the cycle, followed by recovery

Different sectors perform differently at each phase. Consumer staples hold up in recessions; cyclicals like technology and consumer discretionary outperform in expansions.

## Key Takeaway

Macro analysis tells you the direction of the tide. You can swim against it, but it's easier to swim with it. Understanding where we are in the business cycle helps you position your portfolio in sectors that tend to outperform in that environment.
