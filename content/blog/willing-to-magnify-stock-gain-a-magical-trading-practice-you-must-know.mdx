## The Power of Leverage in Markets

The same capital can generate dramatically different returns depending on the instrument you use. A 10% move in a stock becomes a 100%+ move in a correctly structured options position. This is the magic — and the danger — of leverage.

Understanding how to use leverage responsibly is one of the most powerful skills an investor can develop.

## Options: The Most Accessible Leverage Tool

A stock **call option** gives you the right to buy 100 shares at a specific price (the strike price) before a specific date (the expiration). You pay a premium for this right.

**Example:**
- Stock is at $100
- You buy a $105 call expiring in 30 days for $2 (controlling 100 shares = $200 total)
- Stock moves to $115
- Your $105 call is now worth ~$10 = $1,000 profit on $200 investment = **400% return**
- Stock owner: 15% return on $10,000 investment

The same directional bet, dramatically different return profile.

## The Other Side of the Equation

Options are a depreciating asset. If the stock doesn't move enough, fast enough:
- Your option loses value every day (time decay / theta)
- If the stock is below the strike at expiration, you lose 100% of your premium

The same scenario without a move:
- Stock stays at $100
- Your $105 call expires worthless
- **Loss: 100% of the $200 premium**

Options require being right about direction AND timing. This is why most retail options buyers lose money.

## Using Options Responsibly

**Defined risk only:** Only buy options, never sell naked options (unlimited risk exposure). Your maximum loss on a long option is the premium paid — defined and limited.

**Size appropriately:** Options should represent a small percentage of your portfolio (1-3% per trade). The leverage is built in — you don't need large position sizes.

**Choose the right expiry:** At least 30-45 days out. Shorter expiries leave less time for your thesis to play out and accelerate time decay.

**Have a catalyst:** Options work best when you have a specific event in mind — earnings, FDA approval, product launch. Time-decaying instruments need time-bounded catalysts.

## Covered Calls: Earning Income on Your Stock

If you own stock, you can **sell covered calls** to generate income:
- You own 100 shares of a stock at $50
- You sell a $55 call expiring in 30 days for $1 (collect $100 premium)
- If the stock stays below $55, you keep the premium and your shares
- If the stock goes above $55, your shares are "called away" at $55

This is a conservative strategy that lowers your effective cost basis and generates monthly income. Ideal for stocks in your long-term portfolio that you're willing to sell at a higher price.

## Key Takeaway

Leverage is a magnifier — it amplifies both gains and losses. Options give retail investors access to leverage with defined risk, but only when used with discipline: proper sizing, correct expiry selection, and a clear catalyst thesis. Used correctly, they're a powerful tool. Used carelessly, they're a fast path to account destruction.
