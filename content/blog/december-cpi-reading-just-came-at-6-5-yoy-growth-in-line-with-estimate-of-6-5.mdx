## The December CPI Report

The Bureau of Labor Statistics released December's Consumer Price Index (CPI) reading at **6.5% year-over-year** — exactly in line with the consensus estimate of 6.5%. On a month-over-month basis, prices were flat (0.0%), the first time in years that monthly inflation didn't increase.

This was a meaningful deceleration from the June 2022 peak of 9.1% YoY.

## Breaking Down the Numbers

**Categories driving the most change:**
- **Energy:** Down significantly month-over-month, the biggest contributor to slowing inflation
- **Food:** Still elevated at roughly 10% YoY, but the monthly rate of increase is slowing
- **Core CPI (ex-food and energy):** 5.7% YoY — still well above the Fed's 2% target

The important story is the **trend**. CPI peaked in June 2022 and has been declining for six consecutive months. This is the disinflationary trend the Fed wants to see.

## What It Means for the Fed

The Fed's primary tool against inflation is raising interest rates. With inflation clearly trending lower:
- Markets priced in a **25 basis point hike** at the next FOMC meeting (down from the 75bps hikes seen in mid-2022)
- The "pivot" narrative strengthened — investors began pricing in rate cuts later in 2023
- Terminal rate expectations (the peak Fed Funds Rate) stabilized around 4.75–5.0%

## Market Reaction

Equity markets initially rallied on the in-line print. A "no surprise" CPI reading reduces uncertainty — and markets dislike uncertainty more than they dislike bad news.

**Winners in a decelerating inflation environment:**
- **Growth stocks / Tech:** Their valuations benefit from lower discount rates
- **Bonds:** Rising prices as yields fall
- **Consumer discretionary:** Lower inflation improves real purchasing power

## What to Watch Next

- **Core Services Inflation:** The stickiest component. Wage growth is the primary driver — watch the monthly jobs report.
- **Owner's Equivalent Rent:** A major component of CPI that lags real-time housing data by 12-18 months.
- **Fed Communication:** Speeches from Fed officials will signal whether one in-line CPI print changes their outlook.

## Key Takeaway

An in-line CPI print is good news precisely because it wasn't a surprise. The disinflationary trend is intact. For investors, this supports the case for gradually adding risk back to portfolios — but the Fed remains data-dependent and one print doesn't change policy.
