## What is Paper Trading?

**Paper trading** is simulated trading using virtual money — you practice buying and selling stocks with realistic market prices but no real capital at risk. The term comes from the pre-digital era when traders would write down hypothetical trades on paper to track performance.

Modern paper trading platforms use real-time market data, making them nearly identical to real trading — without the financial consequences.

## Why Paper Trade Before Investing Real Money?

**Test your strategy:** Does your approach actually work in practice? Paper trading reveals execution challenges that theory misses.

**Build confidence:** Experience helps. Placing your first "real" trade is less scary when you've already done it 50 times in simulation.

**Learn the platform:** Understand order types, position management, and portfolio tracking without the pressure of real losses.

**Track your decision-making:** Paper trading creates a record of your trades — why you entered, what happened, what you'd do differently.

## How to Get the Most Out of Paper Trading

**Trade with realistic position sizes:** If you plan to invest $10,000 in real life, don't place $50,000 paper trades. Practice the actual allocation you'll use.

**Treat it seriously:** Paper trading only works if you apply the same discipline you'd bring to real money. Don't take wild risks because "it's just paper."

**Journal your trades:** Write down your thesis for each trade before entering. Review your winners and losers — look for patterns.

**Test specific strategies:** Use paper trading to test a specific strategy (e.g., momentum breakouts, earnings plays) before allocating real capital.

**Set a time limit:** Run your paper portfolio for 30-90 days before going live. This captures a meaningful sample of market conditions.

## From Paper to Real Trading

When you're ready to transition:
- Start with your smallest conviction positions
- Never risk more than 1-2% of your account on a single trade
- Keep paper trading alongside real trading to test new strategies risk-free

## Key Takeaway

Paper trading is the closest thing to a "free education" in investing. Use it to build skills, test strategies, and develop the discipline you'll need when real money is on the line. Two minutes to set up; potentially years of value.
