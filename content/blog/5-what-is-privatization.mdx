## What is Privatization?

**Privatization** (also called "going private") is the process of converting a publicly traded company into a privately held one. The company's shares are bought out and removed from the stock exchange — ending public trading.

This is the opposite of an IPO (Initial Public Offering), where a private company goes public.

## How Does a Company Go Private?

The most common methods:

### 1. Leveraged Buyout (LBO)
A private equity firm (or a group of investors) buys all outstanding shares, typically using a combination of equity and significant debt. The debt is secured against the company's assets and future cash flows.

Famous examples: Dell Technologies (2013), Hilton Hotels (2007).

### 2. Management Buyout (MBO)
The company's own management team buys out public shareholders, usually with private equity backing. Management believes they can run the company better without the pressures of quarterly earnings reports.

### 3. Tender Offer
An acquirer offers to buy all shares directly from shareholders at a premium to the current market price — bypassing the board.

### 4. Merger with a Private Entity
The public company merges with a private company, with the combined entity operating as a private business.

## What Happens to Shareholders?

When a company goes private, public shareholders are typically:
- **Paid a cash premium** above the current market price (usually 20-40% premium)
- Required to **tender their shares** — they cannot keep holding the stock

This is generally good news for existing shareholders, as they receive immediate value above market price.

## Why Do Companies Go Private?

- **Escape short-term pressure:** Public companies face relentless quarterly earnings scrutiny. Private companies can focus on long-term strategy.
- **Reduce costs:** SEC filings, investor relations, and compliance are expensive.
- **Operational flexibility:** Restructuring is easier without public shareholder scrutiny.
- **Unlock value:** Management or private equity may believe the company is undervalued by the market.

## Key Takeaway for Investors

If you own shares in a company that announces it's going private, you will almost always receive a **premium buyout price**. This is typically a positive event for shareholders. However, once delisted, you lose the liquidity and transparency of public markets — which is why the premium is offered.
