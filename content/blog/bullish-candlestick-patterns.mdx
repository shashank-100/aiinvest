## What are Candlestick Patterns?

Candlestick charts display price action using "candles" — each showing the open, high, low, and close for a given period. When candles form specific shapes or sequences, they signal potential price reversals or continuations.

**Bullish candlestick patterns** suggest buying pressure is building and prices may move higher.

## The Most Reliable Bullish Patterns

### 1. Hammer
A hammer forms after a downtrend. It has a small body at the top and a long lower shadow (at least 2x the body length), with little or no upper shadow.

- **What it means:** Sellers pushed price down sharply during the period, but buyers stepped in and pushed it back near the open. Strong rejection of lower prices.
- **Confirmation:** Next candle closes higher

### 2. Bullish Engulfing
A two-candle pattern: a small bearish (red) candle followed by a larger bullish (green) candle that completely engulfs the previous candle's body.

- **What it means:** Bears were in control, then bulls completely overwhelmed them. A powerful shift in momentum.
- **Best at:** Support levels, after extended downtrends

### 3. Morning Star
A three-candle pattern:
1. Large bearish candle (strong selling)
2. Small-bodied candle or doji (indecision)
3. Large bullish candle (strong buying)

- **What it means:** Selling pressure exhausted, uncertainty, then buyers take control. One of the most reliable reversal patterns.

### 4. Piercing Line
A two-candle pattern where a bearish candle is followed by a bullish candle that opens below the prior close but closes above the midpoint of the previous candle.

- **What it means:** Bears gap down but bulls fight back strongly. Incomplete engulfing but still significant.

### 5. Three White Soldiers
Three consecutive bullish candles, each opening within the prior candle's body and closing higher than the previous close.

- **What it means:** Sustained buying over multiple periods. Very strong bullish signal, especially after a downtrend.

## How to Use These Patterns

Candlestick patterns are most powerful when they appear:
- **At key support levels**
- **After extended downtrends** (oversold conditions)
- **With higher-than-average volume**
- **Confirmed by the next candle**

Never trade a pattern in isolation. Combine with support/resistance levels, moving averages, and volume for higher-probability setups.

## Key Takeaway

Bullish candlestick patterns are tools, not guarantees. They show you where buying pressure emerged — but you still need context. A hammer at major support with high volume is far more meaningful than a hammer in the middle of a range.
