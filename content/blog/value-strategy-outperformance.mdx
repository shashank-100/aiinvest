## The Value Factor: 90 Years of Evidence

Value investing — buying cheap stocks relative to their fundamentals — is the most studied factor in financial economics. Academic research dating back to Benjamin Graham and David Dodd in 1934, formalized by Fama and French in 1992, consistently shows that value stocks outperform the broader market over long periods.

The "value premium" — the excess return of value stocks over the market — has been documented across:
- Every major developed market (US, Europe, Japan, UK)
- Multiple time periods (pre and post-publication of the research)
- Different value metrics (P/B, P/E, EV/EBITDA, P/FCF)

## Why Value Works

**Behavioral explanation:** Investors overreact to negative news, pushing prices below intrinsic value. Patient investors who buy during these periods of pessimism are rewarded when sentiment normalizes.

**Risk explanation:** Value stocks are often in financial distress — they carry more risk. The higher return compensates for that risk. (The debate between these explanations continues in academic circles.)

**Competitive dynamics:** Value stocks often face less competition from growth-focused investors who are all chasing the same high-momentum names.

## AInvest Value Strategy Construction

The AInvest value strategy doesn't simply buy the cheapest stocks — that approach falls into "value trap" territory. Instead, it screens for:

**Quality filter (first):** ROE >10%, positive FCF, debt-to-equity &lt;1.0
This eliminates genuinely distressed businesses that are cheap for good reason.

**Valuation filter:** P/E below sector median, P/B &lt;2.0, EV/EBITDA below 3-year average
This identifies businesses trading below their historical and peer valuations.

**Catalyst filter:** Positive earnings revision or technical momentum signal
This helps time the entry — avoiding buying into falling knives.

## The Cycle of Value

Value outperforms in cycles:

- **Value underperformed 2010-2020:** The longest growth/momentum run in history, driven by zero interest rates and tech dominance
- **Value dramatically outperformed 2021-2022:** Rising rates and inflation favored value sectors (energy, financials, industrials)
- **Current environment:** With rates higher, value factors remain supported

The key insight: **value's underperformance in any given period does not invalidate the long-term case** — it simply means the factor is "on sale" and due for reversion.

## Performance Statistics

Historically, value factor portfolios have delivered:
- 2-4% annual excess return over the market (before fees)
- Better drawdown protection during market crashes
- Stronger dividends and buyback yield

Combined with quality and momentum filters (as AInvest does), the historical excess return increases to 4-7% annually with improved Sharpe ratio.

## Key Takeaway

The value premium is real, durable, and accessible. The AInvest value strategy captures it systematically — with quality and timing filters that prevent the value trap problem. In a market dominated by momentum and growth, systematic value exposure is a genuine diversifier and return enhancer.
