## Recessions Are Normal — And Survivable

A recession (technically defined as two consecutive quarters of negative GDP growth) has occurred roughly every 7-10 years on average throughout US market history. They are painful but temporary. The S&P 500 has recovered from every single one.

The investors who come out ahead are not those who predicted the recession — they're the ones who were positioned correctly before it arrived and stayed disciplined through it.

## How Stocks Perform in Recessions

On average, the S&P 500 falls 30-40% during a recession. But the stock market is forward-looking — it typically bottoms **6 months before the recession officially ends**. By the time everyone agrees a recession is happening, the worst of the market decline is often over.

This is why selling everything when a recession is confirmed is usually the wrong move. The time to reduce risk is before, not after.

## Defensive Sectors That Hold Up

### Consumer Staples
People still buy groceries, personal care products, and cleaning supplies regardless of the economy. Companies like Procter & Gamble, Coca-Cola, and Walmart have relatively stable revenues in downturns.

### Healthcare
Medical needs don't disappear in recessions. Pharmaceutical companies, hospital networks, and medical device companies tend to be recession-resistant.

### Utilities
Electricity, water, gas — essential services with regulated, predictable revenues. High dividends provide income while you wait for growth to return.

### Dividend Aristocrats
Companies that have raised dividends for 25+ consecutive years have proven they can generate consistent free cash flow through multiple economic cycles.

## Offensive Opportunities in Recessions

The best stock purchases of the last century were made during recessions:
- Buying banks in 2009 returned 5-10x in the following decade
- Buying travel stocks in March 2020 returned 3-5x by 2021

**Cyclical stocks at the trough** — beaten-down companies in cyclical industries (housing, autos, banks, retail) can return multiples when the cycle turns.

## Portfolio Positioning Checklist

**Reduce risk:**
- Trim overvalued, high-P/E growth stocks
- Reduce speculative positions and penny stocks
- Increase cash allocation to 10-20%
- Reduce leverage to zero

**Add defense:**
- Increase consumer staples and healthcare exposure
- Add dividend-paying stocks
- Consider short-duration bonds or TIPS

**Prepare offense:**
- Build a watchlist of cyclical stocks you'd buy at lower prices
- Keep dry powder (cash) ready to deploy at the trough

## Key Takeaway

Recessions don't destroy wealth — panic does. Investors who stay diversified, hold quality companies, and have the conviction to buy during the panic consistently outperform those who flee to cash and miss the recovery. Prepare now, execute with discipline when fear peaks.
