## What are Penny Stocks?

**Penny stocks** are shares of small companies that trade at low prices — typically under $5 per share, though some define them as under $1. They trade on major exchanges like Nasdaq and NYSE, as well as over-the-counter (OTC) markets.

Despite their reputation, penny stocks can offer significant opportunities — and equally significant risks.

## Why Traders Are Attracted to Penny Stocks

**Accessibility:** A small account can buy thousands of shares. A $1,000 account can buy 2,000 shares of a $0.50 stock.

**Volatility:** Penny stocks can move 20%, 50%, even 100%+ in a single day. These moves are impossible in large-cap stocks.

**News catalysts:** A small company winning a contract, announcing a partnership, or reporting earnings surprise can double the stock. The same news in a large-cap might move it 2%.

## The Real Risks

**Pump and Dump Schemes**
Fraudsters buy large positions in thinly traded penny stocks, generate hype through social media and newsletters, then sell while retail investors buy. The stock collapses. This is illegal but widespread in OTC penny stocks.

**Lack of information:** OTC penny stocks have minimal reporting requirements. You may not be able to verify basic financial information.

**Illiquidity:** Some penny stocks trade only a few thousand shares per day. You can buy in but struggle to get out at a reasonable price.

**Dilution:** Many small companies continuously issue new shares to raise cash, diluting existing shareholders.

## How to Identify Legitimate Penny Stock Opportunities

Focus on **exchange-listed** penny stocks (Nasdaq, NYSE) over OTC — they have stronger reporting requirements.

Look for:
- A recent **news catalyst** — earnings beat, FDA approval, contract win
- **Volume spike** — 3–10x normal volume signals real interest
- **Clear chart pattern** — breakout from consolidation on high volume
- **Low float** — fewer shares available means bigger price moves

## Risk Management is Everything

- **Never risk more than 1-2% of your account on a single penny stock trade**
- Set a stop-loss before entering
- Have a profit target — don't get greedy
- Don't hold overnight if you're day trading — gaps can be brutal

## Key Takeaway

Penny stocks are not investments — they are **trades**. Treat them that way. Strict risk management, clear entry/exit rules, and focusing on quality setups (catalyst + volume + chart) separates successful penny stock traders from the majority who lose money.
