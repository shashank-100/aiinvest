## Using Financial Indicators to Screen Stocks

AInvest's screener lets you query the market using specific financial indicators — either through direct filter configuration or natural language queries. This guide covers the key financial indicators available and how to use them effectively.

## Valuation Indicators

**Price-to-Earnings (P/E)**
The most widely used valuation metric. Compare against:
- The stock's own historical average
- Industry peers
- The broader market (S&P 500 average P/E)

Query example: "Tech stocks with P/E below 20" or "Stocks trading at P/E discount to their 5-year average"

**Price-to-Sales (P/S)**
Useful for pre-profit growth companies. A P/S below 2 for a growing company is generally considered attractive.

**Enterprise Value / EBITDA (EV/EBITDA)**
More complete than P/E — includes debt in the valuation. Better for comparing companies with different capital structures.

**PEG Ratio (P/E to Growth)**
P/E divided by earnings growth rate. A PEG below 1 suggests a stock may be undervalued relative to its growth.

## Profitability Indicators

**Return on Equity (ROE)**
How effectively is management using shareholder equity to generate profits? ROE >15% is generally considered strong.

**Return on Invested Capital (ROIC)**
The most important profitability measure for long-term investors. ROIC >10% (and greater than cost of capital) indicates a business with genuine competitive advantage.

**Gross Margin / Operating Margin / Net Margin**
Track margin trends. Expanding margins indicate pricing power or operating leverage — a hallmark of quality businesses.

## Growth Indicators

**Revenue Growth (YoY and QoQ)**
Year-over-year growth shows the annual trend. Quarter-over-quarter shows acceleration or deceleration in real time.

**Earnings Per Share (EPS) Growth**
Both trailing (last 12 months) and forward (analyst estimates for next 12 months) EPS growth are available.

**Free Cash Flow Growth**
Often more reliable than EPS because it's harder to manipulate. FCF growing faster than earnings is a quality signal.

## Balance Sheet Indicators

**Debt-to-Equity Ratio**
How much debt does the company carry relative to equity? Lower is generally safer, though some industries (utilities, REITs) operate with higher leverage by design.

**Current Ratio**
Current assets / current liabilities. Above 1.5 indicates the company can comfortably meet short-term obligations.

**Interest Coverage Ratio**
EBIT / interest expense. How easily can the company service its debt? Above 5 is healthy; below 2 is a warning sign.

## Combining Indicators for Powerful Screens

The most effective screens combine multiple indicators:

**Quality Value screen:** ROE >15%, Debt/Equity &lt;0.5, P/E below sector average, positive FCF

**Growth at Reasonable Price (GARP):** Revenue growth >20%, PEG &lt;1.5, expanding margins

**Defensive Income:** Dividend yield >3%, payout ratio &lt;60%, 10+ years of dividend growth, low debt

## Key Takeaway

No single indicator tells the whole story. Use valuation, profitability, growth, and balance sheet indicators together to build a complete picture of a stock's financial health and relative attractiveness.
