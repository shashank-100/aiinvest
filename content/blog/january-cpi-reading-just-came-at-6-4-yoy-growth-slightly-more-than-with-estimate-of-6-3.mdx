## January CPI: A Small Surprise

January's Consumer Price Index came in at **6.4% year-over-year** — slightly above the 6.3% consensus estimate. Month-over-month, prices rose 0.5%, higher than the 0.4% expected.

While the year-over-year number continues to decline from the June 2022 peak of 9.1%, the month-over-month reacceleration caught markets off-guard.

## What Drove the Upside Surprise?

**Shelter costs** remained the biggest contributor, rising 7.9% year-over-year. As discussed in previous reports, this component lags actual market rents significantly — but it keeps the headline number elevated.

**Food at home** rose 0.4% month-over-month — a slight reacceleration after several months of slowing.

**Energy** was flat after several months of declines — removing a key disinflationary tailwind.

**Core Services (ex-shelter):** This is the Fed's most watched component — often called "supercore" inflation. It came in at 0.6% month-over-month, suggesting services inflation remains sticky.

## The Market Reaction

Equity markets sold off on the hotter-than-expected print:
- S&P 500 fell ~0.9%
- 2-year Treasury yields jumped (pricing in more Fed hikes)
- Growth stocks led the decline (rate-sensitive valuations)

The dollar strengthened as rate hike expectations were revised upward.

## Fed Implications

Before this report, markets were pricing in a **terminal rate of ~4.9%**. After the hot print, expectations shifted to **5.1-5.25%** — meaning more rate hikes ahead than previously anticipated.

The Fed's "data dependent" messaging means every CPI report matters. One hotter print doesn't reverse the trend — but it pushed the pivot timeline further out.

## How to Respond as an Investor

**Don't overreact:** One month of data doesn't change the trend. The disinflationary path remains intact.

**Reassess rate-sensitive positions:** If you're heavily exposed to long-duration bonds or high-multiple growth stocks, a "higher for longer" rate environment presents headwinds.

**Watch the next print:** February's CPI will be the key data point to confirm whether January was a blip or a trend change.

## Key Takeaway

A slightly hot CPI print is a reminder that the path to 2% inflation is not linear. January's number likely reflects seasonal patterns and base effects as much as genuine reacceleration. Stay focused on the 3-6 month trend rather than reacting to single month data points.
