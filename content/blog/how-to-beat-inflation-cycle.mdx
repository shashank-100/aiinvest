## Why Inflation Hurts Investors

Inflation erodes the purchasing power of your cash and fixed-income investments. If inflation runs at 7% and your savings account pays 0.5%, you're losing 6.5% of real purchasing power every year.

The good news: the stock market has historically been one of the best long-term inflation hedges. But not all stocks hold up equally. Strategy matters.

## Asset Classes That Beat Inflation

### 1. Equities (Stocks)
Over long periods, stocks have returned 10% annually on average — well above historical inflation rates. But during high-inflation periods specifically, **value stocks and dividend growers** outperform growth stocks.

Why? Growth stocks are valued on future earnings. Higher inflation = higher discount rates = lower present value of future earnings. Value stocks, which trade on current earnings and cash flows, are less sensitive to this.

### 2. Real Assets
- **Real Estate:** Property values and rents tend to rise with inflation. REITs (Real Estate Investment Trusts) provide stock market liquidity with real estate exposure.
- **Commodities:** Oil, gold, agricultural products, and industrial metals often rise with inflation. Energy stocks are particularly strong inflation hedges.
- **Infrastructure:** Toll roads, utilities, pipelines — companies with pricing power tied to inflation indices.

### 3. Treasury Inflation-Protected Securities (TIPS)
US government bonds where the principal adjusts with CPI. If inflation rises, so does your principal. A pure inflation hedge — though returns are modest.

### 4. I-Bonds
US government savings bonds with a variable interest rate tied directly to CPI. Currently offer attractive yields during high inflation. Maximum purchase of $10,000/year per person.

## Sectors That Outperform During Inflation

| Sector | Why It Works |
|---|---|
| Energy | Oil prices drive inflation; energy companies profit directly |
| Materials | Raw material prices rise with inflation |
| Financials | Banks can raise lending rates faster than deposit rates |
| Real Estate | Physical assets appreciate; rents rise |
| Consumer Staples | Pricing power — people always need food and household goods |

## What to Avoid During High Inflation

- **Long-duration bonds:** Fixed payments lose real value as inflation rises
- **High-P/E growth stocks:** Discounting future earnings at higher rates crushes valuations
- **Cash:** The worst performer in real terms during inflation

## Practical Portfolio Strategy

1. **Reduce cash** — hold only what you need for near-term expenses
2. **Add energy and materials** — direct inflation beneficiaries
3. **Tilt toward value** — lower duration, current earnings
4. **Consider TIPS or I-Bonds** for the fixed income portion
5. **Hold dividend growers** — companies that can raise dividends beat inflation over time

## Key Takeaway

High inflation isn't the end of investing — it's a signal to rotate. Move toward assets with pricing power, physical value, or inflation-linked cash flows. The investors who prepared got rich during the 2021-2022 inflation surge. The ones who stayed in cash or long-duration bonds saw their purchasing power evaporate.
