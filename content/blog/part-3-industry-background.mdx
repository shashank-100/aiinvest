## Why Industry Analysis Matters

Individual company performance doesn't happen in a vacuum. A stock's prospects are heavily shaped by the industry it operates in. Even a well-managed company struggles in a structurally declining industry — and even a mediocre company can do well in a rapidly growing one.

Industry analysis is the bridge between macroeconomic forces and individual stock selection.

## The GICS Framework

The **Global Industry Classification Standard (GICS)**, developed by MSCI and S&P, organizes companies into:
- **11 Sectors** (e.g., Technology, Healthcare, Financials)
- **25 Industry Groups**
- **74 Industries**
- **163 Sub-Industries**

Understanding this hierarchy helps you analyze competitive dynamics at the right level of specificity.

## Porter's Five Forces

Michael Porter's framework for analyzing industry competitiveness:

### 1. Threat of New Entrants
How easy is it for new competitors to enter the industry?
- High barriers (capital requirements, regulations, patents) = good for incumbents
- Low barriers = constant threat of disruption

### 2. Bargaining Power of Suppliers
Can suppliers raise prices and squeeze margins?
- Many suppliers = low power (commoditized inputs)
- Few unique suppliers = high power (e.g., TSMC in semiconductors)

### 3. Bargaining Power of Buyers
Can customers demand lower prices?
- Many alternatives = high buyer power (retail commodities)
- Few alternatives = low buyer power (specialized software, enterprise contracts)

### 4. Threat of Substitutes
Can customers switch to a different product that serves the same need?
- High substitution risk = caps pricing power (taxis vs ride-sharing)
- Low substitution risk = durable pricing power (insulin, utilities)

### 5. Competitive Rivalry
How intensely do existing players compete?
- Oligopoly (few players) = rational competition, better margins
- Perfect competition = price wars, thin margins

## Sector Rotation and the Business Cycle

Different sectors outperform at different stages of the economic cycle:

| Cycle Phase | Outperforming Sectors |
|---|---|
| Early recovery | Consumer Discretionary, Financials |
| Mid-cycle expansion | Technology, Industrials |
| Late cycle | Energy, Materials |
| Recession | Consumer Staples, Healthcare, Utilities |

Understanding where you are in the cycle helps you rotate into the sectors best positioned for the current environment.

## Analyzing Industry Growth

Key questions to ask about any industry:
- **Total Addressable Market (TAM):** How large is the opportunity?
- **Growth rate:** Is the industry expanding or contracting?
- **Penetration rate:** How much of the TAM is already captured?
- **Regulatory environment:** Are regulations a tailwind or headwind?

## Key Takeaway

Industry analysis narrows your stock selection to the right competitive arena. Find industries with strong tailwinds, high barriers to entry, and rational competition — then find the best operator within that industry. That combination is where long-term outperformers are born.
